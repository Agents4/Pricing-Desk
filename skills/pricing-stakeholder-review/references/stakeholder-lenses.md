# Stakeholder lenses for a pricing proposal

Each lens is a reading of the proposal by someone who was not in the room. A lens reports what that person would ask, object to, or need. It is an inference (I) until a real person confirms it. Names and positions come from the context library only; none are assumed.

| Lens | Asks | Objects when | Needs from us |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finance | What is the revenue effect, in which period, and how is it recognized? What is the margin against the floor? | Numbers are not computed, effect is not time-phased, or a concession hides in a discount line | Computed waterfall, period split, floor check, revenue recognition question listed |
| Sales | Can I sell this? What do I say to a customer who pays less today? | The structure is hard to explain, fences are unclear, or the band leaves no room to trade | One-paragraph explanation, give-get options, a clear walk-away |
| Customer procurement | Why this unit? What am I buying, and what may I do with it? What happens at renewal? | The metric is not visible to them, the right granted is unclear, or the price moves with no notice | Plain unit, stated right, notice terms, a price they can forecast |
| Product manager and Legal | Do we hold what this right needs? Which contract clause governs? | A right is priced before the question is asked; a clause is interpreted by pricing | Rights hand-off row, clause questions listed, no assessment by pricing |
| Competitor | How would a rival answer this? | We claim a competitor price with no source or date | Sourced and dated comparison (public pages only) or "unverified" |
| Engineering and metering | Can we meter this unit reliably, by key, by customer, in time to bill? | The metric cannot be measured, or measurement changes the price | Metering definition, data source, lead time, error handling |

Rules for the review:
1. Run the lenses the proposal touches; say which were skipped and why.
2. Each lens returns: its top question, its top objection, and what would change the proposal.
3. Do not invent a person's view. Write "as [role] might read it" and tag I.
4. If a real person has given a view, quote it with date and source, and tag F.
5. Where lenses conflict, show the conflict. The Council decides.
